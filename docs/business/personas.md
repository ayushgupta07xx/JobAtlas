# JobAtlas — User Personas

Three primary personas anchor JobAtlas product and prioritisation decisions. Each is a job-seeker archetype representing a distinct segment of the Indian tech talent market. Market sizing below mirrors the `Persona_Sizing` sheet of the executive workbook (`docs/business/JobAtlas_Executive_Workbook.xlsx`); figures are modelled top-down estimates, not measured usage.

**Sizing model:** `TAM per persona (₹ cr/yr) = addressable population × reachable % × modelled annual value per reachable user`.

---

## 1. Anjali — The Fresh Graduate

> *"I've applied to forty jobs across five sites and I still can't tell which ones I'm actually a fit for."*

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age / stage | 22, final-year / recent B.Tech (Tier-2 college, Indore) |
| Segment | Fresh graduate |
| Current role | None — seeking first full-time role, off-campus |
| Target comp | ₹3–6 LPA entry-level |
| Tech proficiency | High digital native; new to job portals and to evaluating her own fit |
| Devices | Mobile-first; applies from her phone between classes |

**A day in the life.** Anjali checks Naukri, LinkedIn, Instahyre and three college WhatsApp groups every morning. The same fresher openings appear two or three times under slightly different titles. She applies broadly because she can't tell a strong fit from a weak one, hears back from almost none, and has no reference point for what a fair fresher salary even is in Bangalore versus Pune. By evening she's spent two hours portal-hopping and isn't sure she found anything new.

**Goals**
- Land a first role quickly without missing relevant postings scattered across portals.
- Understand which skills employers are actually asking for so she can prioritise learning.
- Get a realistic sense of entry-level pay by city and role.

**Pain points**
- Fragmentation: the same search must be repeated on every portal.
- Duplicate listings waste her time and inflate the apparent market.
- No fit signal — she can't gauge match quality before applying.
- Salary opacity at the fresher level.

**How JobAtlas serves her.** One unified search across all sources with duplicates collapsed; a resume-match score that tells her where she's genuinely competitive; the skill-demand explorer to target upskilling; and entry-level salary views by city where data exists.

**Market sizing**

| Addressable population | Reachable % | Annual value/user | TAM |
|---|---|---|---|
| 320 lakh | 8.0% | ₹600 | **₹153.6 cr** |

Largest pool, lowest per-user value — high-volume, price-sensitive segment.

## 2. Rohit — The Career Switcher

> *"My title says 'Support Engineer' so the portals never show me the analyst roles I'm qualified for."*

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age / stage | 29, 4 years' experience, mid-transition |
| Segment | Career switcher (e.g. support / ops → data analyst) |
| Current role | Employed full-time, job-hunting on the side |
| Target comp | ₹8–15 LPA in the target function |
| Tech proficiency | Moderate–high; comfortable with portals, frustrated by keyword mismatch |
| Devices | Laptop in the evenings; time-poor |

**A day in the life.** Rohit knows his transferable skills map to a data-analyst role, but keyword search keeps returning his current job family. He manually translates job descriptions to figure out whether his SQL and reporting experience counts, and he can't easily see whether switching means a pay cut or a bump. He has maybe forty minutes a night and wastes most of it reformulating searches.

**Goals**
- Surface roles that value transferable skills, not just exact-title matches.
- Quantify the salary delta of switching functions.
- Identify the one or two skills worth investing in to close the gap.

**Pain points**
- Keyword search penalises non-linear careers.
- No semantic understanding of adjacent-skill fit.
- Unclear compensation for the target role versus his current one.
- Limited time to research.

**How JobAtlas serves him.** Embedding-based semantic match surfaces transferable-skill fits beyond literal title matches; salary views let him compare current and target roles by city; the match explanation shows which of his skills are driving each result.

**Market sizing**

| Addressable population | Reachable % | Annual value/user | TAM |
|---|---|---|---|
| 140 lakh | 10.0% | ₹900 | **₹126.0 cr** |

Mid-size, highly motivated pool — strongest engagement (10% reachable) and mid per-user value.

## 3. Sneha — The Senior Hire

> *"I'm not actively looking, but I'll move for the right role — I just don't want forty irrelevant recruiter pings to find it."*

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age / stage | 38, 13 years' experience, Senior/Lead |
| Segment | Senior hire (passive candidate) |
| Current role | Employed, selective, open to the right move |
| Target comp | ₹30–60 LPA |
| Tech proficiency | High; values signal over volume; privacy-conscious |
| Devices | Laptop; browses deliberately, infrequently |

**A day in the life.** Sneha rarely opens a job portal. When she does, senior roles are buried under hundreds of junior listings, compensation at her band is opaque, and recruiter spam makes discreet browsing impossible. She'd rather see five genuinely strong, well-matched senior roles than scroll through two hundred.

**Goals**
- Discreetly discover a small set of high-fit senior roles.
- Benchmark compensation at senior bands.
- Evaluate a company's full set of openings, not one listing in isolation.

**Pain points**
- Senior roles diluted by entry/mid listings.
- Compensation opacity at senior levels.
- Recruiter noise and lack of privacy.

**How JobAtlas serves her.** High-precision match returns few, strong results ranked by score; senior-band salary views where data exists; company aggregation shows a firm's complete set of openings across sources in one place.

**Market sizing**

| Addressable population | Reachable % | Annual value/user | TAM |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 lakh | 6.0% | ₹1,500 | **₹54.0 cr** |

Smallest, hardest-to-reach pool, highest per-user value — low-volume, premium segment.
